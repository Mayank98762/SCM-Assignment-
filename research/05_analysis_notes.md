## A. Five Forces (industry level, locked)

Industry: commercial cargo-handling port services at Indian gateways, FY26 to H1 FY27.

| Force | Container | Dry bulk | Liquid | Overall | Decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | **HIGH** | MODERATE | LOW–MOD | **MOD–HIGH, rising** | APSEZ container share 45.9→44.4% (Q2 YoY); JNPA +11.9% TEU; APSEZ domestic utilisation ≈69% (spare capacity); JSW to 400 MTPA; coal −6%; east-coast margin 50→41% |
| Entry | MOD | MOD | LOW–MOD | **MODERATE** | Greenfield barriers are high (capital, draft, coastal clearance, litigation), but the state is an entrant (Vadhavan: ₹76,220 cr; ~23.2m TEU at full build). Landlord PPPs let operators enter without owning a port. |
| Suppliers | MOD | MOD | MOD | **MOD → HIGH (Mundra, near 2031)** | Grantor (GMB) controls renewal and royalty; Indian Railways holds the haulage monopoly (APSEZ rail TEU −6.4%); crane OEMs (generic) |
| Buyers | **HIGH** | LOW–MOD | LOW | **MOD–HIGH** | Global lines choose port calls; MSC and CMA CGM are JV partners whose rights end in 2031; no external customer ≥10%; 16% of revenue comes from group companies (captive) |
| Substitutes | LOW | MOD (coal transition) | LOW | **LOW–MOD** | No real alternative for EXIM sea trade; falling demand for imported thermal coal |

**Locked verdict:** the industry is **moderately attractive** for incumbents holding good sites. Container gateways on the west coast are becoming less attractive towards 2030 as state-led and private capacity arrives. Bulk and liquids remain protected by captive and contracted demand. High EBITDA margins across operators (APSEZ domestic 72%, GPPL ~61%, JSW ~49%) partly reflect capital intensity (GFA ≈4.1x revenue), not excess profit alone.

## E. Integration (locked)

**Core finding:** the resource that funds the group (R1) is the one most exposed to the two strongest external pressures: grantor power around 2031 and state-led container capacity (Vadhavan). The Ansoff vectors are mostly placed where APSEZ has parity (logistics, marine, international). So the strategy **spreads risk but dilutes returns** (implied FY31 margin 56.8% vs 59%). That trade-off is rational only if new-segment ROCE rises above the cost of capital.

**Contradiction log**

| # | Apparent contradiction | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | High container buyer power vs a 72% domestic margin and revenue per tonne +9% | Cargo mix; captive harbour services (90%); container terminals in JVs, deconsolidated (AICTPL margin ~57%) |
| 2 | "Network" vs non-Mundra volume flat | It is a portfolio, not a network |
| 3 | "Disciplined capital allocation" vs capex overshoot | Leverage discipline yes; *planning* discipline no |
| 4 | "Average tenure 30+ yrs" vs Mundra 2031 | The average is not weighted by EBITDA |

**Scale:** a temporary advantage, because capacity can be matched (JSW to 400 MTPA; Vadhavan Phase 1 ≈15m TEU vs APSEZ's annualised domestic ≈13m TEU). What is sustained is site, land and time, not size.

**Scenarios 2031**

| Scenario | Effect |
|---|---|
| A. Renewal on similar terms | Advantage persists |
| B. Renewal with higher revenue share / conditions | Mundra margin compresses; diversification gains value |
| C. Re-tender or partial | Severe; JV partners' bargaining power peaks in the run-up |

Vadhavan arriving early (≈2030) amplifies B and C for containers.
