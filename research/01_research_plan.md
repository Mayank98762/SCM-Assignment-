## 3. Central argument v2 (provisional; rejection tests listed)

> APSEZ's durable advantage is **concentrated in the Mundra complex**, not spread across a pan-India "network". Mundra combines a concession, a contiguous SEZ land bank, deep draft and shipping-line JV terminals. The rest of the domestic portfolio is currently **not growing** (non-Mundra volume flat in H1 FY27, east-coast margins falling). Group growth is increasingly carried by international ports, marine and logistics, which earn **8–13% ROCE against 23%** in domestic ports. The advantage also rests on a **time-limited legal base**: the Mundra concession, and the co-terminus JV sub-concessions, run to **16 February 2031**, and several supplementary agreements remain unsigned. The strategic question is whether APSEZ's organisation and capital allocation are turning a **site-specific, tenure-contingent advantage** into durable returns elsewhere, or **diluting** them.

**This argument is rejected or revised if:**
- (a) non-Mundra ports show sustained growth over a longer series;
- (b) new segments' ROCE clears WACC with a credible path to domestic-port levels;
- (c) evidence shows real network effects across ports;
- (d) primary evidence shows the Mundra extension secured on comparable terms.

## 4. Five Forces design (done first, at industry level)

**Industry:** commercial cargo-handling port services at Indian gateways. **Segments:** container / dry bulk / liquid. **Catchments:** north-west, west-south, east, plus transshipment.

| Force | India-specific evidence to assemble | Segment notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | Major (915.17 MMT) vs non-major (≈753 MMT) split; JNPA terminal operators; JSW expansion (capacity to 400 MTPA by FY30, secondary); APSEZ container share trend; capacity utilisation (APSEZ domestic ≈69%, i.e. 451/653) | Container ≠ bulk |
| New entrants | Concession and land access; CRZ and environmental clearance; Vadhavan (₹76,220 cr; Phase 1 2029/2030; litigation); state as entrant; Indian Ports Act 2025 | State entry lowers the barrier |
| Supplier power | Indian Railways (haulage monopoly); concession grantor (GMB/SMB/MPA: royalty, revenue share, renewal), adapted and justified (M5); cranes/OEMs; labour (private vs major-port practices) | Grantor power peaks near 2031 |
| Buyer power | Shipping lines that are also JV partners (MSC CT3, CMA CGM CT4); alliances; no external customer ≥10%; related-party sales 19.8%; switching costs by segment | Co-opetition note |
| Substitutes | Route/product substitutes: DFC-enabled alternative gateways (overlaps with rivalry; classified carefully), pipelines for liquids, overseas transshipment, long-run decline of thermal-coal imports | Kept apart from VRIN-N |

**Output:** a rating per force per segment, a headline industry-attractiveness verdict, and a locked summary paragraph.

## 5. VRIN design (inside-out)

**Resources:**
- R1 Mundra complex
- R2 Non-Mundra portfolio
- R3 Transshipment positions (Vizhinjam, CWIT)
- R4 Shipping-line JV partnerships
- R5 Hinterland rail/MMLP/ICD assets
- R6 Asset-light logistics
- R7 Marine and dredging fleet
- R8 Project execution and acquisition integration
- R9 Financial capacity
- R10 Digital (parity control)

**Tests:**
- **V:** evidence of value (margin/ROCE, or a threat neutralised).
- **R:** who else holds it in India.
- **I:** Dierickx & Cool (1989) mechanisms (time compression, asset-mass efficiencies, interconnectedness) and Barney's historical conditions.
- **N (redefined):** "Can a rival deliver the same strategy with a different bundle, and who holds that bundle?"

Each cell records the evidence ID and its independence tag.

## 6. VRIO design and VRIN/VRIO reconciliation

**Outcomes:** parity / temporary advantage / sustained advantage / unused advantage.

**§6.3 reconciliation:** VRIN's explicit N test vs VRIO's imitation-plus-substitution and O test. The plan names the resources where the two frameworks give *different* verdicts (expected: R1, because of tenure; R6, because of O; R9, because of rarity) and explains why.

## 9. Recommendation protocol

Every recommendation must meet all of these:
1. Cite findings from at least 3 frameworks plus the financial module.
2. Not already announced (otherwise labelled "modify/endorse").
3. Pass the SAF screen.
4. Give capital as a range from disclosed data, or mark it unquantified.
5. Have KPIs with a baseline (data-log ID) and a target.
6. State what evidence would prove it wrong.
7. "Sequence/slow" options are allowed.

**Candidate themes, not commitments:**
- Concession-tenure risk management
- Segment capital-allocation gates
- Container-share defence before Vadhavan arrives
- Fixing or recycling the non-Mundra portfolio
- Related-party acquisition governance
